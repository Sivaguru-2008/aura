The quantum layer, measured.

What is served, what it costs on real hardware, and — stated first, not in a footnote — what it does not buy. Nothing here is transcribed by hand; the page fetches /v1/quantum/evidence, which reads each generating script's own output. A missing artifact renders as “not measured”, never as a plausible default.

01

What is actually running

The circuit on the serving path, and the modules that exist in the repo but are not on it. Listed together so the second list cannot be mistaken for the first.

02

Measurement as a clinical instrument

Quantum precision is bought, not given: Var[⟨Z⟩] = (1 − ⟨Z⟩²) / n_shots. Sequencing that budget splits “unsure” into two states with opposite clinical instructions — the one capability here with no classical counterpart.

03

Real hardware, and where the error comes from

An unattributed error figure is not evidence. Three rungs isolate sampling noise from decoherence, which decides whether the lever is more shots or a shorter circuit.

04

Circuit cost, engineered

Two-qubit gates dominate the error budget and the depth on a device that is not fully connected. Transpiled against the real coupling map — no QPU time spent.

05

What the quantum layer does not buy

Reported here, at the same size as everything else, because a benchmark you only publish when it flatters you is not a benchmark.

06

Design space

Qubits × layers × entangler topology. Sorted by accuracy; the two-qubit gate column is the cost that transfers to hardware unchanged.

07

Learned evidence coupling

If the entangled register were doing nothing, pairwise coupling would be flat. Darker cells couple more strongly.
